The Nature of the Realm

The Realm is shaped by established canon, recorded history, and the consequences of every action taken within it. Every character, house, and event contributes to a shared history.

Players are expected to understand and respect the foundations of the Realm before taking actions that may alter its course. The world is not a blank slate; it is a living archive of blood, ambition, and consequence.

What qualifies as canon?

Canon consists of established lore, recorded history, recognized traditions, and the rules governing the Realm.

What counts as canon-based action?

A canon-based action must be supported by established lore, historical precedent, or recognized House tradition.

What does not count?

Actions that ignore House limits, contradict established lore, or bypass RP and authority systems are not canon.

Characters & Immersion

Lore characters must remain grounded in their origin, background, and circumstances. Powergaming and unjustified additions are prohibited. Personal personalities, interests, and relationships are permitted when they remain reasonable for the character and the setting.

Character realism

Extraordinary traits, bloodlines, abilities, or influence require proper justification and approval.

Roleplay conduct

During official RP, players are expected to remain in character and act according to their character's knowledge, status, and circumstances.

Breaking immersion

Introducing concepts that do not belong within the setting or acting on impossible knowledge can break immersion.

Knowledge & Power

Metagaming is the use of information a character could not reasonably know. Legitimate IC sources of information include decrees, ravens, messengers, and direct IC communication.

What is allowed?

Public decrees, direct IC communication, and official rumors passed through proper channels may be known to the wider Realm.

What is not allowed?

If a message or raven was not sent to a character, that character cannot act on its contents.

Balance of power

Conflict must remain fair and meaningful. A powerful House cannot simply declare war on a weaker House without credible cause.

Events & Story Progression

Lore events drive the history of the Realm. They allow Houses, factions, and characters to shape the world, gain influence, face consequences, and leave their mark on history.

Event Type Standard
Wars, battles, sieges Must respect political cause, House standing, and Realm mechanics.
Political gatherings Must be IC, lawful, and consistent with established lore.
Coronations, weddings, feasts Must preserve the culture, ceremony, and political stakes of the setting.

Combat, Injury & Death

Combat has consequences. Characters are expected to roleplay injuries realistically according to their severity. Serious injuries may affect combat ability, inheritance, office, command, or political standing.

Injuries

Temporary injuries may restrict what a character can reasonably do until recovery is established.

Permanent consequences

Long-term injuries may create lasting disabilities or reduce a character's ability to function in society.

Death

Death is permanent unless a specific approved lore mechanism permits otherwise.

Prison, Exile & Justice

Capture, imprisonment, exile, and execution are legitimate consequences of IC conflict and political action.

Prison

A character may be imprisoned if validly captured through war, coup, or political conflict.

Ransom

Notable prisoners may be bargained for compensation, prisoners, land, or alliance terms.

Exile

House exile or exile in place of death may be chosen where circumstances permit.

Execution

Serious offenses may result in execution when lawful authority and process exist.

Houses, Families & Bloodlines

Houses form the foundation of the Realm's political and social structure. Their influence depends on ambition, standing, and the limitations established by the Realm.

House Type Rule
Minor House Lower authority, strict caps, and limited political influence.
Paramount House Wider reach, stronger military and political standing.
Crown House Exclusive royal legitimacy and sovereign authority.

Politics

The Realm is a constant struggle of bloodline, legitimacy, pressure, and survival. Thrones, oaths, and claims exist within a larger structure of law and consequence.

Interregnum

When the Throne is vacant, the realm enters a period without a lawful reigning monarch.

Rule of succession

Inheritance, legitimacy, and rivalry are all governed by established law and recorded precedent.

House accountability

Houses are responsible for the actions of their Highborn and the consequences those actions create.

Marriage, Alliances & Scandal

Marriage is not simply a personal matter; it is political, dynastic, and often dangerous. Alliances tie Houses together without erasing their separate identities.

Political marriages

Valid marriages require public declaration, acknowledgment by both Houses, and compliance with applicable rules.

Reputation

Reputation functions as a real resource. It may be won, lost, or weaponized.

Rumor & scandal

Rumors are not automatically canon. They become significant only when recognized by evidence, law, or official record.

Messaging & Decrees

Ravens and decrees are the official methods by which law, news, and command travel across the Realm. They are the arteries of the political world.

Ravens

Ravens are the recognized form of long-distance communication and are considered authentic when properly delivered.

Proclamations

Noble decrees and proclamations can shape title transfer, succession, loyalty, and war.

Public declarations

Public declarations may alter legitimacy, dynastic standing, and political claims when properly acknowledged.

Retcons & Historical Record

History within the Realm is persistent and consequential. A retcon is a formal correction to the historical record, not a convenient rewrite of realities that a player dislikes.

Correcting errors

Retcons may be used to fix genuine administrative errors, contradictions, or invalid historical assumptions.

What cannot be retconned

A retcon may not erase lawful wars, valid executions, rightful enthronements, or significant consequence without a proper basis.

Finality

Once recognized, major events become part of the official record and remain part of the Realm's memory.

Supernatural & Mystical Lore

The Realm may contain whispers of the supernatural, ancient fear, and unresolved mysteries. However, the supernatural is not a casual power system. It is rare, dangerous, and carefully controlled.

Existence

Elements such as ancient prophecy, unusual bloodline claims, divine omen, or forbidden lore may exist within the setting.

Approval requirement

Any supernatural element introduced into active play must be approved by the Administration before it may be recognized as canon.

No spontaneous claim

Characters may not simply declare dragon blood, magical power, or mystical lineage without evidence, approval, and proper setting justification.

Lore Department & Administration

The Lore Department and the Administration serve distinct but connected functions. Together they preserve continuity, interpret the rules of the setting, and ensure that the Realm remains coherent.

Lore Department

The Lore Department is responsible for maintaining historical consistency, interpreting canon, and deciding whether a claim or event fits established lore.

Administration

The Administration is the final authority over the rules, interpretation, and enforcement of all Realm matters. Its decisions are binding.

Authority split

The Lore Department reviews continuity and lore. The Administration resolves disputes, enforces rulings, and approves exceptional cases.

Agency Role Power
Lore Department Continuity, record, lore interpretation Interpretive authority
Administration Final rule enforcement and dispute resolution Final authority

Final Principle

The Realm is not a place for convenience. It is a place for consequence. Every title carries weight. Every oath can be broken. Every bloodline can be challenged. Every war can change the shape of the world.
