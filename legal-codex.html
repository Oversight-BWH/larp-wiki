Classification of Offenses

Classification Scope Typical Sanctions
Minor Offense Limited harm, low-level disruption Fine, warning, short detention, corrective service
Major Offense Serious harm or deliberate wrongdoing Fines, imprisonment, seizure, judicial penalties
Serious Offense Threat to safety, order, or authority Judicial review and significant punishment
Capital Offense Highest crimes against the Crown or realm Execution only after lawful judicial process

Law Enforcement Authority

Jurisdiction determines who may enforce the Law. Authority is tied to office, territory, and lawful command—not simply to rank or personal influence.

City Watch

Primary authority over public streets, markets, public buildings, and ordinary criminal matters.

House Guard

Lawful authority within a House’s domain and controlled properties.

Kingsguard

Protects the Crown, royal family, residences, and royal security matters.

The Crown

Highest executive authority, subject to the legal limits of the Realm.

Major Crimes

CR-001 — Assault

Unlawful use or attempted use of force without lawful justification. Aggravated when weaponized or targeted at a surrendered, protected, or vulnerable person.

CR-002 — Theft

Unlawful taking of property. Severity rises with value, repeated conduct, or organized criminality.

CR-003 — Murder

The unlawful killing of another with deliberate intent or criminal culpability. Lawful self-defense and battlefield actions remain distinct.

CR-004 — Treason

Deliberate betrayal of the lawful Crown or Realm, including attempts to overthrow the Crown or aid enemies of the Realm.

CR-005 — Kidnapping

Unlawful seizure or confinement of another person against their will. Aggravated if directed at a noble, royal, or vulnerable person.

CR-006 — Abuse of Authority

When an official uses lawful power for private, retaliatory, discriminatory, or otherwise improper ends.

CR-007 — Bribery & Corruption

Offering, accepting, or arranging illegal benefits in exchange for misuse of public authority.

CR-008 — False Accusation

Knowingly making a false criminal charge to secure punishment, detention, or reputational harm.

Punishment Principles

Punishments should be proportionate, lawful, and guided by the nature of the offense. The courts may use warnings, fines, confiscation, imprisonment, exile, removal from office, corporal punishment, or execution when the Law allows it.

Factor Effect
Aggravating factors Premeditation, abuse of office, repeated offending, vulnerability of victim, obstruction of justice
Mitigating factors Voluntary confession, cooperation, coercion, limited harm, restitution
Legal limit Sentencing must remain within the authority granted by Law
