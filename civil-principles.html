Legal Immunities

Certain offices grant limited protection from ordinary arrest or interference, but immunity is not the same as innocence. It is a legal shield against certain forms of action, not permission for wrongdoing.

Royal immunity

Members of the reigning Crown may be shielded from ordinary local arrest unless the Crown consents or lawful judicial process applies.

Diplomatic immunity

Formal envoys and recognized diplomats are protected while performing lawful diplomatic functions, but not while committing crimes or violence.

Judicial immunity

Judges and officers acting lawfully within their office are protected from personal punishment for lawful decisions. The shield falls when corruption or abuse occurs.

Defensive Zones

Some locations receive special legal protection to preserve order, security, and dignity. Defensive zones are created to protect certain institutions, but they are not absolute sanctuaries from the Law.

Examples

  • Royal residences
  • Courts
  • Major religious sites
  • Government buildings
  • Diplomatic zones

Common restrictions

  • Violence may be limited
  • Weapons may be regulated
  • Special authorization may be required for arrests
  • Combat may be prevented unless necessary to stop immediate harm

Civil Protections

All subjects enjoy foundational legal protections under the Law. These protections ensure that order is not secured through arbitrary force or political intimidation.

  • Protection against arbitrary imprisonment
  • Protection against unlawful seizure of property
  • Protection against unlawful violence by officials
  • Protection from punishment without lawful grounds
  • Protection against denial of a lawful judicial process
  • Requirement of lawful basis before serious action is taken
