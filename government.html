The Crown

The Crown is the highest sovereign authority in the Realm. The reigning King or Queen holds ultimate legal authority over matters affecting peace, foreign relations, royal appointments, decrees, and the preservation of royal order.

Royal authority is not an absolute personal power. It is lawful authority derived from succession, recognized institutions, and the Law itself. The Crown may command the Realm, but it does not stand apart from the law it serves.

What the Crown May Do

  • Issue royal decrees
  • Appoint or remove officials
  • Command the Kingsguard
  • Grant pardons and amnesties
  • Recognize treaties and foreign agreements

What the Crown May Not Do

  • Ignore established succession law
  • Rewrite the legal order arbitrarily
  • Strip subjects of lawful rights without cause
  • Conceal personal misconduct behind royal authority

The Small Council

The Small Council acts as the principal advisory and administrative body of the Crown. Its purpose is to help the monarch govern efficiently and ensure that royal authority is exercised through capable and lawful institutions.

Councillors advise the Crown within their areas of expertise, administer their offices, and coordinate royal matters across the Realm. They do not replace the Crown, and they may not issue royal decrees or exercise sovereign authority without lawful authorization.

Typical offices

  • Hand of the King/Queen
  • Master of Laws
  • Master of Coin
  • Master of Ships
  • Master of Whisperers

Removal and discipline

  • Misconduct
  • Abuse of authority
  • Disloyalty
  • Incompetence
  • Criminal conduct

The Kingsguard

The Kingsguard exists to protect the Crown, the Royal Family, and those placed under direct royal protection. Every sworn member bears a duty to the Crown above private interests, House allegiance, or personal ambition.

The office carries great responsibility. Kingsguard members may enforce lawful royal orders and protect royal institutions, but they may not act as private agents of a House or obey orders that would directly violate the Law.

The City Watch

The City Watch is the primary civilian peacekeeping and law-enforcement institution within the King's cities. Its purpose is to keep the peace, enforce public order, investigate crime, and defend citizens from unlawful violence or disorder.

The King's Peace protects the public from unlawful disorder and criminal misconduct. Breaking it may be an offense even where no private House dispute exists. Nevertheless, Watchmen possess no unrestricted power and must act within lawful grounds and reasonable force.

Authority and Limits

Institution Primary Jurisdiction Typical Authority
The Crown Realm-wide Executive authority, royal appointments, decrees, pardons
Small Council Royal administrative matters Advice, office administration, governance coordination
Kingsguard Crown and royal security Protection, direct commands, royal enforcement
City Watch Cities and public order Patrol, detention, investigation, public peace
Lords and Ladies Territory and estates Local law, guard authority, local governance
